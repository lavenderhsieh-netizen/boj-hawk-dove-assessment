## Important caveat, up front

The BOJ's Summary of Opinions is explicitly **anonymous by design** — each Policy Board member and government representative submits a word-limited summary of what they said, and the Governor edits/orders the final published list without names attached. The Bank has never confirmed a speaker-to-bullet mapping, and there is no way to verify one after the fact except when a member later self-references their own SOO language in a subsequent speech.

Everything below is **informed inference**, built from three things this dashboard already tracks:

1. **Direct quotes** — each current member's own 2026 speeches/press remarks (`data.json → speakers/board`), several of which use near-identical phrasing to specific July SOO bullets.
2. **The known vote** — July was 8–1; Takata Hajime was the sole dissenter, proposing a hike to 1.25%. Everyone else, including the board's other declared hawk (Tamura), voted for the hold.
3. **BOJ's own editorial convention** — within each SOO subsection, opinions are typically sequenced from more cautious/consensus-toned to more assertive, ending on the most pointed dissenting-style logic. That ordering lines up with this project's own hawk-dove `spectrum` scores (Takata 2.5 > Tamura 2.0 > Koeda 1.5 > Ueda/Himino 1.0 > Masu 0.5 > Uchida 0.0 > Sato −0.5 > Asada −1.5), which is corroborating evidence, not proof.

Confidence is graded **High / Medium / Low** per bullet. Treat High as "language match is close enough that I'd bet on it," Medium as "consistent with their known lean, several plausible authors," Low as "no strong signal either way." Government-representative bullets (Ministry of Finance, Cabinet Office) are excluded — those aren't Policy Board members and the SOO already labels which government body they're from.

## I. Economic Developments — bullet by bullet

**1. "Japan's economy has recovered moderately... partly due to the Middle East... fiscal 2027 growth rises as effects wane."**
→ **Ueda (Medium).** This is the SOO's own opening/baseline sentence — it reads as the chair's synthesis framing rather than one member's individual submission, and echoes Ueda's repeated "appropriately" / balanced-outlook style.

**2. "Economy experiencing an upswing driven by the AI demand shock... concerns over an economic slowdown have subsided."**
→ **Tamura or Masu (Medium).** Confidently optimistic framing, consistent with the hawk camp; Masu's own quotes lean on "government measures" and steady optimism about normalization, Tamura leans on demand-shock language.

**3. "For economic activity, Middle East downward, AI demand upward, yen depreciation works in both directions."**
→ **Sato (Medium-High).** This is a genuinely two-sided, three-factor balancing statement — almost a textbook match for Sato's own inaugural framing ("watch for both downside risks to growth and upside risks to inflation"). The near-identical sentence reappears in the Prices section and again in the Monetary Policy reaction-function bullet, suggesting one analytically-minded member's framework got picked up as the SOO's organizing device for the whole document — plausibly Sato's, or the Governor's own synthesis built around it.

**4. "AI-related demand has spread to a greater extent than expected... wealth effects from higher stock prices."**
→ **Koeda (Medium).** Matches her "Meeting with Local Business Leaders" reporting style and her data-driven tag; she has previously cited local-business-meeting evidence directly.

**5. "Due attention... economy could deviate downward, should AI-firm profitability diminish and stock market adjust."**
→ **Asada or Sato (Medium).** Textbook dove hedge. Asada's own June dissent leaned on downside risks to production/employment; this bullet is more market/AI-equity-specific, which fits Sato's newer, more markets-aware framing slightly better, but either is plausible.

**6. "Japan has experienced [past] declines in domestic demand from external shocks... but shown resilience against U.S. tariffs and the Middle East."**
→ **Himino (Low-Medium).** Historical/comparative framing fits his "steady gradualist" analytical style; no strong direct-quote match though.

**7. "Cabinet approved the Basic Policy... 'responsible and proactive public finances'... raise production, employment, income, inflation."**
→ **Masu (Medium).** His quotes repeatedly reference government measures/fiscal support alongside monetary normalization.

**8. "Since June, a significant regime shift... rate-cut phase globally has turned to a rate-hike phase."**
→ **Takata or Tamura (Medium).** Global-synchronization framing is a recurring hawk theme (both have referenced Japan's rate stance relative to global peers); can't separate the two on this bullet alone.

## II. Prices — bullet by bullet

**1. "Underlying CPI likely to increase gradually... generally consistent with target between H2 FY2026 and FY2027."**
→ **Ueda (Medium-High).** "Generally consistent with the price stability target" is close to his own repeated June/July phrasing.

**2. "Approaching 2 percent... important to examine whether it becomes anchored at around 2 percent."**
→ **Koeda (High).** Near-verbatim match to her own quote: "underlying inflation is about 2 percent."

**3. "For prices, Middle East/AI demand/yen depreciation all exert upward pressure."**
→ **Sato or Ueda (Medium)** — same three-factor framework as Economic Developments #3 above; likely the same author.

**4. "Domestic distribution costs, packaging materials, food trays... price hikes accelerate toward early autumn."**
→ **Koeda (Low-Medium).** Granular, data-specific pricing detail consistent with her data-driven tag; no direct quote to confirm.

**5. "Middle East unclear; oil/naphtha declined from April peak; PPI risen but CPI remained below 2 percent partly due to government policy."**
→ **Asada (Medium).** The emphasis on CPI still sitting below 2% is a dove-favoured framing — consistent with a member building the case for patience.

**6. "Oil supply/demand balanced... tankers exited the Persian Gulf... could tighten again once temporary factors dissipate."**
→ **Unclear (Low).** Technical commodity-market observation; could be Uchida (market-functioning remit) but no strong signal.

**7. "Yen depreciation, geopolitical/climate risks pushing up import prices; strong AI demand could also lift goods/services prices; attention warranted on upside risks."**
→ **Tamura (Medium-High).** Multi-factor upside-risk stacking is consistent with his consistently-most-hawkish-voice tag.

**8. "Oil pass-through and Japan's output gap... risks to prices significantly skewed to the upside... AI demand and expansionary fiscal policies abroad could push prices up even more."**
→ **Takata (High).** The single most emphatic upside-risk-skew bullet in the Prices section — this is his signature "don't fall behind the curve" logic, and the fiscal-policy-abroad reference matches his global-comparison style.

## III. Monetary Policy — bullet by bullet

**1. "Financial conditions accommodative — short-term real rate negative, lending attitudes proactive, loan growth accelerating."**
→ **Himino (Medium).** Standard technical BOJ-staff-style assessment; fits his measured, analytical deputy-governor role.

**2. "Necessary to also assess whether financing is being implemented under disciplined investment plans."**
→ **Uchida (Medium).** A financial-stability/prudential caveat — squarely his remit as the deputy governor who oversees market operations and financial-system health.

**3. "Time lag of ~1–1.5 years for a hike's effects to emerge... appropriate to hold this meeting."** *(the explicit hold rationale)*
→ **Ueda (High).** This is the decision statement's own stated reason for holding — it is very likely the Governor's own framing of the majority consensus, not one dissenting or hawkish voice.

**4. "Given underlying CPI approaching 2%... appropriate to continue to raise the policy rate and adjust the degree of accommodation."**
→ **Himino (Medium).** Matches his own quote almost exactly: policy "should gradually shift to a more neutral stance through moderate policy rate hikes."

**5. "Consider timing and pace... examining the Middle East, AI demand, and FX... attention to upside risks to the underlying price trend."**
→ **Koeda (Medium-High).** Matches her own May speech closely: sees "possibility inflation may exceed 2% given the Middle East situation," favours raising rates "at an appropriate pace."

**6. "Perspective of stabilizing underlying CPI around 2 percent... keep the risk of deviating upward from materializing."**
→ **Ueda (Medium-High).** Consistent with his repeated "thoroughly discuss" / target-stabilization framing.

**7. "Necessary to carefully monitor whether medium- to long-term inflation expectations, which have been rising recently, stabilize around 2 percent."**
→ **Himino or Koeda (Low-Medium).** Analytical/monitoring tone; no strong distinguishing signal between the two.

**8. "Greater consideration to upside risks than before... pace of hikes could be faster than market expectations."**
→ **Tamura (High).** Near word-for-word match to his own 25 June remark: "should quicken the pace given stronger inflation risks."

**9. "Inflation becoming anchored around 2 percent... attention to upside risks... adjust the policy rate nimbly."**
→ **Takata (High).** "Nimbly" is his signature word (also appears in bullet 10 below); consistent with his history of proposing specific faster hikes.

**10. "Even if the precise neutral rate cannot be determined... raise the rate, which is below the lower bound of the broadly estimated range... ensure the nimbleness of policy decisions."**
→ **Takata (High).** Same "nimbleness" language; his actual dissent (1.25%) is consistent with treating the current 1.00% rate as still below any reasonable neutral-rate floor.

**11. "Global environment turning to a hike phase... discuss the size of a rate hike, rather than adhering to a certain pace... shift from encouraging a rise in inflation to preventing upward deviations."**
→ **Takata (High).** "Discuss the size of a hike" is essentially a direct paraphrase of what a member proposing 1.25% (a 25bp jump rather than the incremental path) would submit. This bullet reads as the closest thing to Takata's own dissent rationale appearing in the anonymous text.

**12. "Should upside risks to prices materialize... compel rapid and substantial hikes later, causing a double shock... necessary to accelerate the pace of adjustment."**
→ **Tamura (Medium-High).** "Double shock" (delay now → bigger shock later) is the same overshoot-avoidance logic as his own quote: "need to hike to neutral to avoid an overshoot." Plausible this is Tamura's own written opinion — hawkish enough to read like a dissent, but he ultimately voted with the majority in July (a "soft dissent in writing, majority vote in practice" pattern that would be consistent with his board record).

**13. "Long-term interest rates rose in early July; policies in various countries attracted attention as a factor influencing daily market movements."**
→ **Uchida (Medium).** Market-functioning/technical observation, his usual remit.

## What this exercise actually tells you

Stripping out the individual-bullet guesswork, three things are fairly robust even under the "this is speculative" caveat:

1. **Takata's fingerprints are on at least 3-4 of the most hawkish bullets** (the "nimbly"/neutral-rate-floor pair, and the "discuss the size of a hike" bullet) — consistent with a dissenter whose written submission argues the case in full even though it lost the vote 8-1.
2. **Tamura's language shows up in the SOO's hawkish tail without him dissenting** — his own June quotes map cleanly onto 2-3 of the most upside-risk-skewed Monetary Policy bullets, which is the textbook shape of a member who agreed to hold procedurally (transmission-lag argument) while still wanting his hawkish reasoning on the record. Watch him as the next plausible dissenter if September data firms up.
3. **The dove counterweight (Asada, Sato) shows up mainly in the Economic Developments and Prices sections** (downside-AI-equity risk, CPI-still-below-2% framing) rather than in Monetary Policy — neither of them appears to have submitted an outright hold-forever argument this time, which is itself a signal: even the board's two most dovish members' own SOO-adjacent language leans on *conditional* caution ("if AI profitability diminishes," "CPI still below 2% partly due to government policy") rather than an unconditional case against hiking. That is consistent with September staying live rather than the dovish wing building a blocking coalition.

Read together with the `july_vs_june_2026.md` verdict (July SOO is more hawkish underneath the hold), this speaker exercise suggests the hawkish tilt is not just Takata talking loudly — Tamura's language is doing real work in the text too, even though only Takata put his name on a dissent.
