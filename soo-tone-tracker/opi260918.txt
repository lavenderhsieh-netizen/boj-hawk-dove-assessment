             Summary of Opinions at the Monetary Policy Meeting1,2
                        on September 17 and 18, 2026

I. Opinions on Economic and Financial Developments
Economic Developments
⚫   Japan's economy has recovered moderately, although some weakness has been seen in part,
    partly due to the impact of the situation in the Middle East. Regarding the outlook, Japan's
    economy is expected to continue growing moderately. This is because, although the impact of
    the situation in the Middle East is expected to push down economic activity, the economy is
    likely to be underpinned by factors such as the government's various measures, in addition to
    an increase in global AI-related demand.

⚫   The somewhat weak figures for domestic demand in the April-June GDP statistics are
    considered to be attributable to technical factors regarding the treatment of, for example, the
    sale of patent rights abroad and the provision of free school lunches. Overall, both economic
    activity and prices have been developing generally in line with the projections made in the July
    2026 Outlook for Economic Activity and Prices, and attention continues to be warranted on
    upward deviations in prices, more so than on downward deviations in economic activity.

⚫   Although the real GDP growth rate for the April-June quarter was positive, the breakdown
    shows that the growth rate for domestic demand was negative, and while external demand was
    positive, this was merely the result of a decrease in imports reflecting supply-side constraints
    stemming from the situation in the Middle East. Thus, it cannot be said that the economy is
    growing in a strong and sound manner.

Prices
⚫   Underlying CPI inflation is likely to increase gradually, as the mechanism in which wages and

⚫   Underlying CPI inflation has been fairly close to 2 percent. Among the relevant indicators,
    there are some exceeding 2 percent and others falling below it. However, it is difficult to
    determine conclusively whether underlying CPI inflation has reached 2 percent based on an

II. Opinions on Monetary Policy
⚫   Japan's economic activity and prices have been developing generally in line with the baseline
    scenario, and underlying CPI inflation has been approaching 2 percent. Under these
    circumstances, financial conditions have been accommodative, and attention is warranted on
    upside risks to prices. In view of such developments, it is appropriate for the Bank to raise the
    policy interest rate and adjust the degree of monetary accommodation.

⚫   It is appropriate for the Bank to raise the policy interest rate, since (1) economic and price
    developments are largely on track; (2) the references used to determine the timing of the rate
    hike -- namely, the situation in the Middle East, AI-related demand, and developments in
    foreign exchange rates -- continue to warrant attention as factors that could drive up prices; and
    (3) financial conditions have shown no significant change since the previous rate hike and
    appear to be in accommodative territory.

⚫   While various factors have been cited in the market as reasons for raising the policy interest
    rate, it is necessary for the Bank to explain clearly that the rate has been raised at this MPM in
    light of developments in Japan's economic activity and prices as well as financial conditions.

⚫   If a policy interest rate hike is decided at this MPM, it will be the first one in the three months
    since June, and the interval between rate hikes will be shorter than before. This is because
    economic conditions have been more resilient than expected.

⚫   Given that the year-on-year rate of increase in the CPI has been below 2 percent, and it cannot
    necessarily be said that the recent economic situation is strong, it is desirable for the Bank to
    maintain the current policy interest rate at this MPM.

⚫   Current economic and price developments do not appear to have substantially accelerated
    compared to before, and in this context, it is not appropriate for the Bank to raise the policy
    interest rate at this time.

⚫   As for the future conduct of monetary policy, given that underlying CPI inflation has been
    approaching 2 percent and financial conditions have been accommodative, it is appropriate for
    the Bank to continue to raise the policy interest rate and adjust the degree of monetary
    accommodation, in response to developments in economic activity and prices as well as
    financial conditions.

⚫   With underlying CPI inflation considered to be below 2 percent, the aim of monetary policy
    thus far has been to push it upward. However, underlying CPI inflation is now getting closer to
    2 percent, and the perspective of stabilizing it at a level around 2 percent thus becomes
    important in order to keep the risk of underlying CPI inflation deviating upward to a level
    above the price stability target of 2 percent from materializing and thereby exerting an adverse
    impact on the economy afterward. In that sense, it is considered that there has been a shift in
    the phase of monetary policy.

⚫   It is necessary for the Bank to shift from the current stance of encouraging a rise in underlying
    inflation and to demonstrate to the market its determination to prevent upward deviations in
    prices through a nimble response to overseas economic environments and to price
    developments, while giving consideration to the impact stemming from foreign exchange
    markets.

⚫   If, (1) prices continue to develop in a manner consistent with the price stability target of 2
    percent, and (2) financial conditions remain accommodative, it will be necessary for the Bank
    to continue to adjust the degree of monetary accommodation by raising the policy interest rate.
    If signs of an upward deviation in prices are observed, the Bank will need to accelerate the pace
    of rate hikes.

⚫   With regard to the future conduct of monetary policy, it is desirable for the Bank to bring the
    policy interest rate closer to the approximate goal relatively soon and ensure there is room for
    nimble adjustment in either direction, so that it can respond flexibly to unexpected movements
    in economic activity and prices and to changes in the economic environment.

⚫   Underlying CPI inflation is expected to reach 2 percent before long. It does not seem to be
    accelerating at a speed that could lead the Bank to fall behind the curve, so there is no need to

    take hasty action. However, the Bank should conduct monetary policy as appropriate to prevent
    excessive and persistent price increases.

⚫   When deciding on a future policy interest rate hike, although it is important to keep in mind
    the estimated range of the neutral interest rate, the Bank should continue to act in a timely
    manner without becoming overly cautious, while broadening the scope and improving the
    granularity of its analysis, given that there is a certain degree of risk of price increases.

⚫   Depending on developments overseas, the neutral interest rate may deviate upward from prior
    estimates or the levels anticipated by the market. Rather than making a priori assumptions
    about the neutral interest rate, it is necessary to examine the degree of monetary
    accommodation after each policy interest rate hike by observing factors such as the behavior
    of firms, households, and financial institutions, and financial conditions.

⚫   Firms' views are important in assessing financial conditions. At present, most firms report that
    the impact of past policy interest rate hikes and near-term potential further rate hikes is limited,
    and only a very few firms report that they will refrain from fixed investment due to interest
    burdens. The purpose of policy interest rate hikes is to stabilize prices by influencing demand,
    and it is necessary to continue to comprehensively consider various views and assess the degree
    of monetary accommodation as a whole.

⚫   Financial conditions have remained accommodative. This is partly attributable to the easing
    effects of the monetary accommodation to date being amplified through asset prices.

⚫   Communication with market participants needs to be conducted effectively, in order to prevent
    one-sided monetary policy expectations from being factored into the market.



III. Opinions from Government Representatives
Ministry of Finance
⚫   The government expects the Bank to conduct monetary policy as appropriate toward
    sustainable and stable achievement of the price stability target of 2 percent, while closely
    cooperating with the government.

⚫   On this basis, regarding the proposal on the change in the policy interest rate, the government
    expects the Bank to make decisions at this MPM as appropriate, and to explain its policy
    intention carefully to the market and other stakeholders.

Cabinet Office
⚫   The government expects the Bank to fulfill its accountability regarding the decisions at this
    MPM and to examine carefully the cumulative effects of past policy interest rate hikes. Looking
    ahead, it may be necessary for the Bank to take into consideration its estimates of the neutral
    interest rate. It is important for the Bank to take proactive and appropriate action in the event
    of excessive fluctuations in economic activity and in the market.

⚫   The government expects the Bank to conduct monetary policy as appropriate toward achieving
    the price stability target of 2 percent in a sustainable and stable manner, while closely
    cooperating with the government.
