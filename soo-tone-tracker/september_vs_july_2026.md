---
title: "BOJ Summary of Opinions: September vs July 2026"
---

*Meetings: 17–18 September 2026 (SOO released 1 October) vs 30–31 July 2026 (released 10 August). Source: BOJ official English translations.*

# Bottom line

- **Decision:** July = hold at 1.0% (8–1, Takata dissenting for a hike). September = hike to 1.25% (7–2, Asada and Sato dissenting against).
- **Tone:** Path guidance is more hawkish than July (explicit "accelerate if upside signs", shorter hike intervals, neutral rate may be higher), but the board is visibly less united, with two dovish dissents.
- **Back-to-back hike (October)?** The SOO does not point to it. One member notes this was the first hike in three months and that intervals are getting shorter, but another says there is "no need to take hasty action" and that the Bank is not behind the curve. Quarterly pacing (December next) is the better read.
- **50bp?** No support in the document. The language is conditional: only "if signs of an upward deviation in prices are observed, the Bank will need to accelerate the pace". July had a similar line ("pace could be faster than market expectations"), and a larger hike did not follow.

# Standout opinions

**Most hawkish**

1. "If signs of an upward deviation in prices are observed, the Bank will need to accelerate the pace of rate hikes."
2. "Underlying CPI inflation has generally reached 2 percent. Developments in prices since the previous MPM have further supported this view."
3. Need to "shift from the current stance of encouraging a rise in underlying inflation" and "demonstrate to the market its determination to prevent upward deviations in prices through a nimble response".
4. "The neutral interest rate may deviate upward from prior estimates or the levels anticipated by the market."
5. "Bring the policy interest rate closer to the approximate goal relatively soon."

**Most dovish**

1. "Given that the year-on-year rate of increase in the CPI has been below 2 percent, and it cannot necessarily be said that the recent economic situation is strong, it is desirable… to maintain the current policy interest rate." (consistent with Asada's dissent)
2. "Current economic and price developments do not appear to have substantially accelerated compared to before… it is not appropriate… to raise the policy interest rate at this time." (consistent with Sato's dissent)
3. "Underlying CPI inflation… does not seem to be accelerating at a speed that could lead the Bank to fall behind the curve, so there is no need to take hasty action."
4. Q2 GDP: domestic demand negative, and the positive external contribution reflects lower imports; "it cannot be said that the economy is growing in a strong and sound manner."
5. CPI (ex-fresh food, and ex-fresh food and energy) "have remained in the range of 1.5–2.0 percent", and services price inflation has plateaued.

*The SOO does not name speakers. Linking bullets to the two dissenters is inference from the vote and content.*

# Side-by-side

| Theme | July (hold) | September (hike) |
|---|---|---|
| Rationale | Examine impact of June hike (1–1.5 year lag); keep rate unchanged | Data on track; Middle East, AI demand and FX are upside risks; financial conditions still accommodative |
| Underlying inflation | "Approaching 2%"; check it anchors around 2% | One view "generally reached 2%"; another says undeterminable, so assume "around 2%" and focus on anchoring |
| Price risks | "Significantly skewed to the upside" (oil pass-through, output gap, AI, fiscal, weak yen) | Upside risks "continue to be high"; oil may stay high; distribution costs and packaging help pass-through |
| Growth | AI-led upswing, slowdown concerns subsided, consumption resilient; AI-bubble downside flagged | Q2 domestic demand negative (called technical); not "strong and sound"; Middle East hit smaller than feared |
| Guidance | Hike pace could exceed market expectations; discuss size of hike | Continue hiking if conditions hold; accelerate on upside signs; keep room for nimble adjustment in either direction |
| Neutral rate | Policy rate below lower bound of the range, so hike | Rate now inside lower end of range; neutral could be higher; assess after each hike |
| Pace | Hawks: accelerate | "Interval shorter than before" vs. "no hasty action" |
| Dissent | Takata (hike) | Asada, Sato (hold) |
| MoF | Earthquake, fiscal sustainability | Asks for careful explanation; examine impact of hike |
| Cabinet Office | Fiscal path, market confidence | Examine cumulative effect of past hikes; consider neutral estimates; act if market moves excessively |

# Questions this raises

1. Does the "shorter interval" language imply quarterly pacing is the new baseline, or the upper end of what the board will accept?
2. With two Takaichi-appointed members dissenting, how much does the vote constrain the next hike timing?
3. What evidence would count as "signs of an upward deviation" strong enough to accelerate (core CPI, wage settlements, import prices, yen)?
4. The Cabinet Office is asking for neutral-rate estimates. Will the BOJ publish a refreshed range, and does 1.25% inside the range reduce the urgency?
