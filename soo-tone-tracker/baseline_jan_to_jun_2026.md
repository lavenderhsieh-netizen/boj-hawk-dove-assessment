## Meeting-by-meeting

### January 2026 (post-Dec'25 hike, steady-state)
- Headline: "Japan's economy has recovered moderately, although some weakness has been seen in part... likely to continue growing moderately, supported by... the government's economic measures and accommodative financial conditions..."
- Price risk balance: **"upside and downside risks to prices are generally balanced"** — the most neutral framing of all 4 meetings.
- Hawkish: "it is necessary for the Bank to adjust the significantly negative real policy interest rate"; "should not take too much time examining the impact... should proceed with the next step, a rate hike, without missing the appropriate timing."
- Dovish: "If the pace of policy interest rate hikes is not too rapid, the Bank need not be overly concerned about the impact on firms' business performance."
- No crisis narrative yet. Net: measured hawkish continuation, unhurried.

### March 2026 (Middle East shock enters)
- Middle East conflict / crude oil spike appears for the first time as a first-order risk.
- Risk balance goes two-sided/conditional: "while downside risks remain, there is also the possibility that underlying inflation will exceed 2 percent, depending on the future course of the situation in the Middle East."
- New recurring anxiety motif born here: **"falling behind the curve."**
- Starkest hold-vs-hike split of the four: "the Bank should maintain the current policy interest rate at this meeting" (deteriorated market sentiment) directly against "the Bank will need to raise the policy interest rate without hesitation."
- First "1970s-type stagflation" comparison.
- Net: hawkish-leaning but anxious/two-narrative.

### April 2026 (hold, but Prices section turns most one-sidedly hawkish)
- Headline explicitly folds Middle East into the baseline: "some weakness... partly due to the impact of the situation in the Middle East."
- Prices section becomes the most unambiguously upside-skewed of all 4 meetings: **"all scenarios point to further upside risks to prices"**; extended 1979 oil-crisis comparison, arguing today's conditions are MORE prone to second-round effects than 1979.
- Yet Monetary Policy section shows the most genuine wait-and-see sentiment of the four: "the Bank needs to adopt a wait-and-see approach at this meeting"; "current circumstances do not suggest a pressing need to quickly raise the policy interest rate."
- Widest gap seen anywhere between risk-section language (hawkish) and policy-section conclusion (hold) — classic pre-hike coiled spring.

### June 2026 (THE HIKE — to 1.00%) — freshest baseline for July comparison
- Downside-risk framing flips explicit: **"Downside risks to economic activity have decreased"** — clears the hurdle that held back April's hold.
- Middle East: "an agreement has been reached to end the conflict" — resolving, no longer the dominant open risk.
- New growth/inflation driver introduced: **"Global AI-related demand is pushing up economic activity and prices more than expected."**
- Hike rationale (near-verbatim echo of the Prices section): "there is a risk that price increases stemming from high crude oil prices will spread across a wide range of items, and a risk that underlying CPI inflation will deviate upward to a level above the price stability target of 2 percent, it is appropriate for the Bank to raise the policy interest rate."
- New granular hawkish evidence, first appearance: BEI (break-even inflation) rate shift, credit/asset-price growth from negative real rates, explicit **neutral rate estimate "around 2 percent."**
- Forward guidance added for the first time: **"the Bank should maintain its stance to continue to raise the policy interest rate."**
- Visible dissent: an explicit minority "hold the rate steady" bullet, plus the sharpest dovish line of the whole set — a deflation-relapse warning: "...this could potentially cause Japan's economy... to fall back into deflation again."
- Separate minority dissent on JGB taper: one bullet opposes halting the JGB purchase-reduction (taper) on central-bank-independence/credibility grounds — "there is no reason at all to halt the reduction... this could lead to bias in the market and undermine the credibility of the Bank."
- Net: clearly the most hawkish SOO of the four — tight risk→action causal chain, new hawkish evidence types, explicit forward guidance, real but outvoted dissent.

## What to check when July's SOO lands (10 Aug)

Cross-reference against June's exact phrases:
1. Is **"downside risks to economic activity have decreased"** reaffirmed, softened, or reversed?
2. Does the **"risk... underlying CPI inflation will deviate upward to a level above 2 percent"** phrase persist, weaken (e.g. to "close to" / "generally consistent with" 2%), or strengthen?
3. Is the forward-guidance bullet — **"should maintain its stance to continue to raise the policy interest rate"** — repeated, watered down, or dropped? (Dropping/softening this = the single strongest dovish tell, since July was a hold.)
4. Does the AI-demand-driven growth/inflation narrative grow or fade?
5. Does dissent language persist/intensify — note July's framing will likely flip from "why we hiked" to "why we didn't hike again" (Takata's dissent for a further hike to 1.25% is already known from the July decision statement — check how strongly this is echoed in the anonymous SOO bullets vs. any hold-favoring bullets).
6. Does the neutral-rate estimate ("around 2 percent") get repeated, revised, or dropped?
7. Does yen/FX-specific language re-emerge as a standalone driver now that oil/Middle East has faded — especially relevant given the late-July FX intervention episode.

## Known fact from the July decision statement (already public, 31 Jul)
Board voted 8-1 to hold at 1.00%. Dissenter: **Takata Hajime**, who proposed hiking to 1.25%, arguing "the situation had shifted to a new phase in which the Bank needs to adopt a nimble approach in response to upside risks to prices caused by demand shocks stemming from overseas developments and to changes in overseas financial conditions." Worth checking whether the SOO's anonymous hawkish bullets echo this "nimble"/demand-shock framing almost verbatim (as June's did with its own hike rationale) — if so, that's strong internal-consistency confirmation of whose voice is whose.
