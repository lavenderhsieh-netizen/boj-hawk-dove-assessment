Basic Portfolio revision process — governance & approval flow

To change the Basic Portfolio, GPIF and the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) follow a formal, multi-step approval chain — MHLW is legally required to act in the interests of beneficiaries throughout. GPIF reviews its policy portfolio every year and can in principle revise the Basic Portfolio ahead of the next scheduled five-year review (due 2029) if the Management Committee judges the investment environment has changed enough that GPIF can no longer expect to meet its required real return (nominal wage growth + 1.9%) — as of Aug 2026, officials say a full strategic review is not imminent, but the fund retains discretion to shift within its existing permissible ranges without one.

Why this matters

The policy rate alone does not describe BOJ tightening. Rate normalisation, lower purchases, balance-sheet runoff and private-sector duration absorption are happening together — this tab tracks the pace and composition of quantitative tightening directly from the BOJ's own published accounts.

Total BOJ assets since 2012

JGB (JGS) holdings since 2012

JGS = Japanese Government Securities (JGBs + Treasury Discount Bills/FBs), the BOJ's own balance-sheet line item.

Current-account / reserve balances (¥tn)

Largest liability-side counterpart to BOJ asset creation, but short-term moves also reflect government deposits, lending facilities, repo and banknotes — not a one-for-one mirror of JGB holdings. Rose mechanically as BOJ bought JGBs 2013-2024; now the main liability absorbing QT.

QT decomposition — gross purchases vs. implied redemptions vs. net change (¥tn/mo)

A decline in BOJ JGB holdings can reflect smaller purchases, larger redemption months, or both — this decomposes the two. Redemptions are implied, not BOJ-published: gross outright purchases (DB=MD06) minus that month's net change in JGS holdings. The pattern lines up with Japan's redemption calendar — spikes recur every Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec — the best available check on this approximation.
Bars: gross outright purchases (+) and implied redemptions (−). Line: net change in JGS holdings. Not broken out by maturity bucket — BOJ's daily operation-result releases carry that detail but aren't in a clean scrapable series here.

Gross JGB issuance less BOJ purchases — private-sector absorption requirement (¥tn/mo)

The other side of the QT story: each month MOF issues new JGBs and BOJ buys back some outright — the gap is duration the private sector must absorb. As BOJ purchases taper while issuance stays roughly flat, this gap widens — the mechanism behind rising super-long yields and steeper curves. Gross measure, not true net-duration-supply — see note below.
Bars: MOF gross coupon-JGB issuance (ex-T-Bills) minus BOJ outright purchases (DB=MD06). Line, right axis: BOJ absorption ratio = purchases ÷ issuance. Caveat: gross, not net of redemptions — see card below for the new-money-vs-refunding split.

Non-JGB holdings — ETFs, J-REITs, corporate bonds · secondary to JGB QT, but ETF disposal is politically sensitive

The rest of the BOJ's unconventional-easing legacy. Corporate bonds are unwinding fast (no reinvestment of maturing holdings since 2022); ETFs and J-REITs are barely moving — the BOJ's ETF sale program is real but glacial, which is exactly why it keeps generating political debate about a faster/larger disposal.
"Years to unwind at current pace" is a naive linear extrapolation of each series' trailing-6-month run-rate — not a BOJ target or announced schedule. Treat as a pace indicator, not a forecast: corporate bonds follow a real maturity schedule, while ETF/J-REIT sales have no disclosed cadence.

Retail fund-flow proxy, classified — domestic vs. foreign equity mandate

Diversification, not repatriation. Answers: within the retail fund-flow proxy, how much is Japan-only vs. US vs. ex-Japan vs. global vs. other? Every fund in the Wealth Advisor top-20 list (111 distinct funds, Jul 2024–Jul 2026) is classified by name — domestic-Japan, US, developed-ex-Japan, emerging-market, global all-country (~95% foreign/~5% Japan approximation), global/thematic, non-equity — a first-pass heuristic, imprecise for balanced/thematic products. Don't use this alone to infer NISA's overall domestic/foreign share — NISA also includes direct stocks/ETFs, a different universe (growth-quota direct-stock buying is domestic-tilted but not 100%: JSDA's 2025 White Paper found 9 of the top 10 growth-quota stocks domestic). Read: domestic-equity funds' share of flow was near-zero through 2024-2025, risen to ~20% by mid-2026 — but look through the ~95%-foreign global all-country sleeve (KPIs below) and foreign equity is still ~3/4 of underlying exposure. This is Japan gaining share at the margin within a still foreign-equity-dominated portfolio, not a reversal of overseas diversification.
