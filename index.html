Basic Portfolio revision process — governance & approval flow

To change the Basic Portfolio, GPIF and the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) follow a formal, multi-step approval chain — MHLW is legally required to act in the interests of beneficiaries throughout. GPIF reviews its policy portfolio every year and can in principle revise the Basic Portfolio ahead of the next scheduled five-year review (due 2029) if the Management Committee judges the investment environment has changed enough that GPIF can no longer expect to meet its required real return (nominal wage growth + 1.9%) — as of Aug 2026, officials say a full strategic review is not imminent, but the fund retains discretion to shift within its existing permissible ranges without one.

Why this matters

The policy rate alone does not describe BOJ tightening. Rate normalisation, lower purchases, balance-sheet runoff and private-sector duration absorption are happening together — this tab tracks the pace and composition of quantitative tightening directly from the BOJ's own published accounts.

Total BOJ assets since 2012

JGB (JGS) holdings since 2012

JGS = Japanese Government Securities (JGBs + Treasury Discount Bills/FBs), the BOJ's own balance-sheet line item.

Current-account / reserve balances (¥tn)

Current deposits are the largest liability-side counterpart to BOJ asset creation, but short-term movements also reflect government deposits, lending facilities, repo operations, banknotes and other balance-sheet factors — not a one-for-one mirror of JGB holdings. Rose mechanically as BOJ bought JGBs 2013-2024; now the main liability absorbing the shrinking asset side under QT plus the July 2024 taper plan.

QT decomposition — gross purchases vs. implied redemptions vs. net change (¥tn/mo)

A decline in BOJ JGB holdings can reflect smaller purchases, larger maturity/redemption months, or both — this decomposes the two. Redemptions are implied, not directly published by the BOJ: gross outright purchases (BOJ Time-Series API, DB=MD06) minus that month's net change in JGS holdings. The pattern lines up cleanly with Japan's JGB redemption calendar — large redemption months recur every March/June/September/December, which is the strongest available check on this approximation without the BOJ publishing a redemptions series directly. This one chart replaces what used to be three separate cards here (monthly net change, gross purchase operations, and the chart below it) — each showed a piece of the same story; this shows all three together.
Bars: gross outright purchases (+) and implied redemptions (−, shown as negative for readability). Line: net change in JGS holdings (= purchases − redemptions). Not broken out by maturity bucket — BOJ's daily operation-result releases carry that detail but aren't yet in a clean scrapable time series on this dashboard.

Gross JGB issuance less BOJ purchases — private-sector absorption requirement (¥tn/mo)

The other side of the QT story: every month MOF issues new JGBs and the BOJ buys back some of them outright — the gap is duration the private sector (banks, insurers, pension funds, foreign investors) has to absorb. As BOJ purchases taper while MOF issuance stays roughly flat, this gap widens — the mechanism behind rising super-long yields and steeper curves. This is a gross measure, not a true net-duration-supply figure — see the note below.
Bars: MOF gross coupon-JGB issuance (auction offering amounts, Treasury Discount Bills excluded) minus BOJ outright JGB purchases (DB=MD06). Line: BOJ absorption ratio, right axis = BOJ purchases ÷ MOF gross issuance. Caveat: "gross issuance" here is the full auction offering amount, which includes both new-money financing bonds and 借換債 (refunding bonds reissued to redeem maturing debt) — it is not netted against redemptions on either side of the ledger, so this is a gross absorption requirement, not a true net-new-duration figure. See the card just below for MOF's own new-money-vs-refunding split, which is the more accurate annual basis for net duration supply.

FY2026 MOF issuance plan — new-money vs. refunding · annual, not monthly

The chart above nets MOF's total auction offering amount against BOJ purchases, but most of that gross issuance is 借換債 (refunding bonds) — reissued specifically to redeem maturing JGBs, replacing old paper rather than adding new duration to the market. MOF's own annual issuance plan breaks this out directly; it's the more accurate basis for "true" net new duration, just at yearly rather than monthly granularity, since auctions don't tag individual bonds as new-money vs. refunding.

Balance-sheet contraction pace — 3m / 6m / 12m cumulative change in JGB holdings (¥tn)

Three overlapping windows instead of one annualised rate. The quarterly redemption spikes shown in the QT-decomposition chart above mean a single-quarter annualised contraction rate (×4) mechanically overstates the "true" taper pace whenever it happens to land on a heavy redemption month — the 3m line will look dramatic every Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec even when purchases haven't changed. Free-float JGB supply — BOJ holdings vs. private-sector holdings (¥tn)

The BOJ-ownership-share chart above only shows BOJ's slice shrinking; this reframes the same two numbers (BOJ holdings, total outstanding) as what's actually tradable. QT can mean two things happening at once: the private sector has to absorb more new duration each month (see the absorption-requirement chart higher up), and more of the existing stock circulates in private hands rather than sitting on the BOJ's balance sheet — a liquidity tailwind even as it's a duration headwind. Both are true simultaneously; this chart is the liquidity side of that story.
Private-sector (free-float) holdings = total JGB outstanding (DB=FM05) minus BOJ holdings (DB=BS01). Not a precise "tradable" measure — it still includes JGBs held to maturity by banks/insurers under held-to-maturity accounting, not just actively-traded float — but it is the right direction of travel for market-functioning/liquidity discussions. Same ~1-month FM05 lag as the BOJ-ownership-share chart above.

Non-JGB holdings — ETFs, J-REITs, corporate bonds · secondary to JGB QT, but ETF disposal is politically sensitive

The rest of the BOJ's unconventional-easing legacy. Corporate bonds are unwinding fast (no reinvestment of maturing holdings since 2022); ETFs and J-REITs are barely moving — the BOJ's ETF sale program is real but glacial, which is exactly why it keeps generating political debate about a faster/larger disposal.
"Years to unwind at current pace" is a naive linear extrapolation of each series' trailing-6-month run-rate — not a BOJ target or announced schedule (the BOJ has never committed to a fixed ETF/J-REIT disposal timeline). Treat it as a pace indicator, not a forecast: corporate bonds are winding down on a real, deliberate maturity schedule, while the ETF/J-REIT figures reflect occasional book-value sales with no disclosed cadence, so a straight-line projection is illustrative at best for those two.
