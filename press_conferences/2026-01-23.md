# BOJ Press Conference — 22–23 Jan 2026

**Decision:** Hold at 0.75%  
**Vote:** 8–1 (Takata dissented for 1.0%)  
**Presider:** Governor UEDA Kazuo

---

The BOJ’s inflation outlook has not moved sharply above its previous
forecasts. Compared with the October Outlook Report, the latest inflation
forecast is broadly similar, with only a modest upward revision to
core-core CPI.

The BOJ does not see a situation where actual inflation is greatly
exceeding its forecasts and forcing the Bank to keep revising the outlook
higher. Rather, the December rate hike was based on the view that the
existing inflation outlook was becoming more likely to be realized.

The BOJ remains focused on underlying inflation. Even if headline or core
CPI temporarily falls below 2%, policy decisions will place more weight on
whether underlying inflation continues to rise gradually toward the 2%
target.

The Bank also continues to monitor yen depreciation, long-term interest
rates, fiscal policy, wage-price pass-through, services inflation, and the
impact of previous rate hikes.


## Q&A Transcript

### Q1. Six months ago, you said that if inflation stayed higher than expected, the BOJ could accelerate the pace of rate hikes. Is Japan now in that situation?

**A:**

No. Compared with the October Outlook Report, the latest inflation outlook
is broadly similar. Core-core CPI was revised up slightly, but this is not
a situation where inflation is greatly exceeding our forecast and we are
chasing it higher with repeated upward revisions.

That said, at the December meeting, we judged that the probability of our
existing inflation outlook being realized had increased. That was why we
raised rates.

If the outlook shown today is realized, or if confidence in it rises
further, then it would be appropriate to continue gradually adjusting the
degree of monetary accommodation.

---

### Q2. How do you view central bank independence and the BOJ’s relationship with the government?

**A:**

If the economy and prices evolve in line with our outlook, and confidence
in that outlook rises, then further gradual adjustment of monetary
accommodation would be appropriate.

We will continue to explain this carefully and maintain close communication
with the government.

For the BOJ, the most important thing is to conduct policy properly so that
the 2% price stability target is achieved smoothly and sustainably.

---

### Q3. Is the BOJ becoming more concerned about the impact of yen depreciation on underlying inflation?

**A:**

Yes, we are paying attention to this.

Firms’ price-setting behavior has become more active, so domestic prices
may now react more strongly to exchange-rate movements than before.

Also, as overall inflation and underlying inflation have risen and moved
closer to 2%, even smaller exchange-rate moves may require more attention
than in the past.

---

### Q4. Long-term interest rates have risen quickly. Why has the BOJ not conducted flexible bond-buying operations?

**A:**

We are closely monitoring developments in long-term interest rates while
staying in close contact with the government and taking account of the
respective roles of the government and the BOJ.

We also need to consider that supply-demand conditions in super-long JGBs
have become unstable due to fiscal year-end factors.

---

### Q5. Has the BOJ discussed revising the JGB purchase-reduction plan because of the rise in long-term yields?

**A:**

The Policy Board does discuss flexible operations and regular bond-buying
operations from time to time.

But our conclusion so far is to continue closely monitoring the market,
while maintaining close communication with the government and respecting
the respective roles of the government and the BOJ.

---

### Q6. If headline CPI falls below 2%, can the BOJ still hike rates?

**A:**

Yes. Headline inflation is likely to decline clearly for some time and may
fall below 2%. But underlying inflation is expected to continue rising
gradually.

Our monetary policy decisions will place greater weight on the trend in
underlying inflation. This stance has not changed. We will continue
explaining it carefully.

---

### Q7. Has confidence in achieving the 2% target increased?

**A:**

There are differences of opinion among board members. For example, Board
Member Takata believes underlying inflation is already broadly at 2% and
therefore supported a hike.

On average, the board still sees some distance from 2%, but expects
underlying inflation to rise gradually toward that level.

Compared with the October Outlook Report, confidence has increased.
Compared with December, however, there has not been a large change.

---

### Q8. How important is the April price-revision season for judging wage-to-price pass-through?

**A:**

It is one important factor, but not necessarily the most important one.

Previously, many price increases were driven mainly by raw-material costs.
Recently, however, our hearing information increasingly shows firms citing
wage costs as a reason for raising prices.

April is a month when many firms revise prices, so we will pay attention to
it. But the next rate-hike decision will not depend on that factor alone.

---

### Q9. How would potential consumption-tax cuts affect inflation and the economy?

**A:**

A consumption-tax cut has not been decided, so it is not included in our
current outlook.

If it were implemented, the impact on the economy and prices would be an
important issue. However, the degree of pass-through to prices is
uncertain, and international experience shows different views on this.

For now, I cannot say clearly what the effect would be.

---

### Q10. What will be the key factors for the next rate hike?

**A:**

Unlike the previous hike, this is not a situation where we are focused on
only a few specific checkpoints.

Wages and prices are rising gradually, so we need to judge from a wide
range of indicators whether that process continues and at what pace.

We will also consider how the previous rate hike affects the economy and
prices.

---

### Q11. How strong is services inflation in Japan?

**A:**

In advanced economies where inflation is stable around 2%, services prices
usually rise steadily and play a major role.

Japan has not fully reached that situation yet. Some service prices,
excluding rent and public services, are rising around 2%. But rent outside
major cities has not risen much, and public services are also restrained
for institutional reasons.

Accommodation and dining prices can also move due to temporary factors. We
need to judge carefully how persistent those moves are.

---

### Q12. How long does the BOJ need to assess the impact of the previous rate hike?

**A:**

The final impact on business investment, housing investment, consumption,
and inflation takes a long time to become clear.

But we do not simply wait for final data. We try to detect the impact
earlier by looking at broad financial conditions and by conducting frequent
hearings with firms about investment and spending plans.

The pace of future hikes will depend on how each hike affects the economy.

---

### Q13. Does yen depreciation mean the BOJ needs to hike faster?

**A:**

The pace of rate hikes will depend on economic and price conditions.

We will examine the effects of previous hikes carefully, gather information
early, and judge the impact on the economy. Because the neutral rate is
difficult to estimate precisely, we proceed by assessing the effects of
each hike rather than mechanically targeting a specific distance from
neutral.

---

### Q14. Could fiscal expansion or tax-cut debate affect markets and inflation?

**A:**

Tax policy is decided by the government and Diet.

From the BOJ’s perspective, it is extremely important that the government
secures market confidence in medium- to long-term fiscal sustainability.

---

### Q15. Did government measures change the BOJ’s view of underlying inflation?

**A:**

Government inflation measures support disposable income and consumption,
while growth-promotion measures support the economy. Through stronger
growth, they can have a positive effect on underlying inflation.

We already had this view in December. This time, we quantified the impact
and incorporated it into our outlook.

---

### Q16. If long-term rates rise sharply, what comes first: flexible bond purchases or revising the QT plan?

**A:**

Super-long JGB supply-demand conditions are currently unstable, partly due
to fiscal year-end factors.

We will take that into account, stay in close contact with the government,
and monitor developments while respecting the respective roles of the
government and the BOJ.

---

### Q17. Has the timing of achieving the 2% target been brought forward?

**A:**

It is not possible to discuss the timing with that level of precision.

Some board members believe the target has already been achieved or will be
achieved sooner. But as a board-wide view, we still describe the timing as
the second half of the projection period.

---

### Q18. Are long-term yields rising more than the BOJ intended? Is the BOJ behind the curve?

**A:**

I will not comment on very short-term market moves.

Over recent weeks, market participants have pointed to various factors
behind higher long-term yields, including views on fiscal policy. We have
repeatedly said that it is important for the government to secure market
confidence in medium- to long-term fiscal sustainability.

From the perspective of BOJ independence, our most important role is to
conduct policy properly so that price stability is achieved smoothly.

---

### Q19. How long will headline inflation stay below 2%?

**A:**

Headline inflation is likely to fall below 2% fairly soon.

However, there is still high uncertainty over how long it will remain below
2%. We have not changed our view on the duration.

---

### Q20. Should the government specify funding sources for possible consumption-tax cuts?

**A:**

The detailed explanation of how possible tax cuts would affect medium- to
long-term fiscal conditions, including funding sources, is for the
government to provide.

---

### Q21. Has the recent JGB move become “exceptional” enough to require flexible BOJ action?

**A:**

We will continue to monitor the situation closely while staying in close
contact with the government and considering the respective roles of the
government and the BOJ.

---

### Q22. Does yen depreciation increase the risk of falling behind the curve?

**A:**

We do not believe we are currently behind the curve.

Our outlook is that Japan will reach a situation consistent with
sustainable achievement of the 2% target in the second half of the
projection period.

That said, we will conduct policy appropriately so that we do not fall
behind the curve.

---

### Q23. Will the GDP benchmark revision raise the estimated neutral rate?

**A:**

We have not recalculated it yet. Stock data and other inputs are still
needed.

My expectation is that the new data will not greatly change the
neutral-rate estimate. Once the calculation is ready, we may consider
publishing it.

---

### Q24. Is a high-pressure economy still appropriate when Japan faces labor shortages and high prices?

**A:**

I have not discussed in detail what proponents of a high-pressure economy
specifically mean.

For the BOJ, the key point is that inflation should not overshoot
significantly from the outlook shown today. We will conduct monetary policy
properly to prevent that.

---

### Q25. Does the BOJ agree with the view that the recent bond-market shock has subsided?

**A:**

Volatility remains high, so we will continue to monitor the situation
carefully.
