# BOJ Press Conference — 15–16 Jun 2026

**Decision:** Hike to 1.00%  
**Vote:** 7–1 (Asada dissented for hold)  
**Presider:** Deputy Governor UCHIDA Shinichi *(Governor Ueda was hospitalised)*

---

## Core Takeaways

- The hike was driven by both reduced growth downside risk and increased inflation upside risk.
- The BOJ is shifting from "getting inflation to 2%" toward "stabilising inflation around 2%."
- Future hikes remain likely, but the path is data-dependent and not tied to a fixed six-month pace.
- Neutral rate estimates are too uncertain to guide policy mechanically.
- FX is not a target, but its inflation pass-through is now more important.
- The BOJ wants to avoid falling behind the curve, but no 50bp hike was proposed.
- JGB purchase reductions are being slowed to JPY 2tn/month from April 2027 to preserve market stability.
- The BOJ does not see rate hikes and slower JGB runoff as contradictory.
- ETF sales are not being accelerated for now.
- Governor Ueda's absence did not affect the institutional policy decision.


## Q&A Transcript

### Q1. Why did the BOJ raise rates at this meeting?

**Q:** What was the main reason for today's rate hike?

**A:**

The BOJ judged that downside risks to the economy had declined compared with the previous meeting, partly because alternative procurement of Middle East-dependent raw materials has progressed. The economy is broadly moving in line with the central scenario: growth is slowing but remains moderate.

On prices, although headline CPI is currently below 2%, cost pass-through from higher crude oil prices is moving faster than expected at the corporate-goods level. This could spread to a broader range of consumer prices. Medium- to long-term inflation expectations are also continuing to rise.

Therefore, the BOJ judged that there is a risk that underlying inflation could overshoot the 2% price stability target, and decided it was appropriate to raise the policy rate and adjust the degree of monetary accommodation.

---

### Q2. What is the BOJ's policy guidance from here?

**Q:** How should we think about future rate hikes?

**A:**

As underlying inflation is approaching 2%, and financial conditions remain accommodative, the BOJ expects to continue raising the policy rate and adjusting the degree of monetary accommodation depending on economic, price, and financial conditions.

However, the pace and path of future hikes are not predetermined. The BOJ will continue to monitor Middle East developments and their impact on financial markets, Japan's economy, and prices.

The key point is to stabilise underlying inflation around 2%, not merely reach 2% temporarily.

---

### Q3. If inflation is not yet stably at 2%, why hike now?

**Q:** Japan has not yet achieved stable 2% inflation. Why raise rates before that is confirmed?

**A:**

The BOJ focuses on underlying inflation, which continues to rise gradually toward 2%. The central scenario is that underlying inflation will reach a level broadly consistent with the 2% target from the second half of FY2026 into FY2027.

At the same time, corporate price pass-through is moving faster, and medium- to long-term inflation expectations are rising. Therefore, the BOJ must also pay attention to upside risks — namely the risk that underlying inflation overshoots 2%.

This upside risk was one of the reasons behind today's hike.

---

### Q4. Is Japan entering a wage-price spiral?

**Q:** Is the BOJ worried about a wage-price spiral?

**A:**

The mechanism where wages and prices rise together has become increasingly established, supported by labour shortages.

So far, this looks consistent with 2% inflation rather than destabilising. Base pay increases are around the mid-2% range, and scheduled cash earnings are around the high-2% to 3% range. This is broadly consistent with 2% inflation.

The BOJ does not necessarily use the phrase "good spiral," but the mechanism of wages and prices reinforcing each other in a way consistent with 2% inflation is viewed positively.

---

### Q5. Was the hike driven more by the baseline outlook or upside inflation risk?

**Q:** Was today's hike mainly because the baseline outlook improved, or because inflation upside risk increased?

**A:**

Both factors mattered. The decision was not based on assigning a higher weight to one pillar over the other.

The BOJ looked at all information available at the meeting and judged that both the central outlook and the risk assessment supported a rate hike.

---

### Q6. Does the upside inflation risk imply multiple future hikes?

**Q:** If underlying inflation may overshoot 2%, does that mean multiple hikes are now in sight?

**A:**

The BOJ's basic policy is to continue adjusting monetary accommodation as needed. However, it does not commit to a specific number of hikes.

Neutral interest rate estimates vary widely, so the BOJ cannot say precisely how far the current policy rate is from neutral. In practice, it will raise rates while carefully assessing how financial conditions change and where the neutral level may be.

---

### Q7. How does the BOJ judge neutral rate?

**Q:** Is the BOJ close to the neutral interest rate?

**A:**

Neutral rate estimates are highly uncertain and vary widely. Because the range is so broad, it is not very useful for practical policy management.

The BOJ will therefore explore the neutral level through the actual process of raising short-term rates and observing changes in financial conditions.

---

### Q8. What was Governor Ueda's role despite his absence?

**Q:** Governor Ueda was absent. Did that affect the decision?

**A:**

The content of Governor Ueda's written opinion cannot be disclosed before the usual publication process through the Summary of Opinions and minutes.

However, his broad thinking is consistent with what he has previously said publicly.

His absence did not affect the policy decision. The BOJ operates as an institution, not as one individual. His hospitalisation is understood to be short-term and not expected to have a major impact.

---

### Q9. Why did Governor Ueda not vote remotely?

**Q:** Why did Governor Ueda not participate in the vote remotely?

**A:**

The explanation given was that it was due to the circumstances of his medical treatment.

---

### Q10. How does the Middle East ceasefire affect policy?

**Q:** If the Middle East situation is easing, does that make further hikes easier or more difficult?

**A:**

It is too early to say. The situation may affect both growth and prices.

If geopolitical risks ease, downside risks to the economy may decline, which could make normalisation easier. But lower crude oil prices could also reduce upside inflation pressure, which could argue for more caution.

The BOJ will carefully assess how the situation evolves.

---

### Q11. Could Middle East risks change the timing of achieving 2% inflation?

**Q:** Does the Middle East shock bring forward or delay the timing of achieving 2% inflation?

**A:**

It could work in both directions. Higher energy prices and cost pass-through could bring inflation forward, while negative effects on the economy could delay it.

For now, the BOJ has not changed its main view that underlying inflation will reach the 2% target range from the second half of FY2026 into FY2027.

---

### Q12. How important is FX for policy?

**Q:** Is the yen's movement a policy consideration?

**A:**

FX is not a direct policy target. However, FX movements are an important factor because they affect the economy and prices.

Recently, firms' wage- and price-setting behaviour has become more active. This means FX movements may pass through to prices more easily than in the past. The BOJ will continue to monitor FX and its impact on inflation expectations and underlying inflation.

---

### Q13. How does the BOJ avoid being behind the curve?

**Q:** Is the BOJ already behind the curve?

**A:**

The BOJ does not think it should fall behind the curve. Its current policy framework is to keep raising rates and adjusting monetary accommodation as needed.

The BOJ will manage policy appropriately so that it does not become behind the curve.

---

### Q14. Is the market's concern about "behind the curve" valid?

**Q:** Market moves suggest concern that the BOJ may be behind the curve. How does the BOJ respond?

**A:**

The BOJ does not comment on day-to-day market movements. However, market pricing contains important information about participants' views on the economy, prices, and the policy outlook.

The BOJ's policy objective remains price stability. It will continue adjusting monetary accommodation so that it does not fall behind the curve.

---

### Q15. Is a faster pace than one hike every six months possible?

**Q:** Markets have assumed roughly one hike every six months. Is that still the right pace?

**A:**

The BOJ does not commit to a fixed pace. Future decisions will depend on economic, price, and financial conditions.

In the near term, Middle East developments remain important. More broadly, the BOJ will assess whether the economy and prices are on track, including upside risks to inflation.

---

### Q16. Was a 50bp hike discussed?

**Q:** Did anyone propose a 50bp hike?

**A:**

There was no 50bp hike proposal.

---

### Q17. Why hike now rather than at the previous or next meeting?

**Q:** Why was this meeting the right timing?

**A:**

The biggest change from the previous meeting was that downside risks to the economy had declined.

Alternative procurement of raw materials has progressed, and government measures to reduce energy burdens are expected to help. Corporate earnings remain very high, and employment and income conditions continue to improve.

On prices, domestic corporate goods prices have risen, reinforcing awareness of inflation upside risk. But the main difference versus the previous meeting was the reduction in economic downside risk.

---

### Q18. How effective is a 25bp hike?

**Q:** Is a 25bp hike enough to contain inflation?

**A:**

A 25bp hike itself affects the economy and prices. But today's hike should be seen as part of a broader policy path, not as a standalone move.

The BOJ has also indicated that it will continue raising rates as needed. Through this sequence of policy adjustments, the BOJ aims to guide inflation toward stable 2%.

---

### Q19. Why did the statement remove "real rates are extremely low"?

**Q:** The statement no longer says real rates are extremely low. Does this mean policy is closer to neutral?

**A:**

This was mainly a change in explanation, not a change in judgment.

The BOJ wants to describe policy more logically by focusing on overall financial conditions, not only real interest rates or the policy rate level. The BOJ still judges that financial conditions remain accommodative, and that rate hikes are needed to adjust the degree of accommodation as underlying inflation approaches 2%.

---

### Q20. How long can the BOJ keep saying it is only "adjusting accommodation"?

**Q:** At some point, won't the BOJ move out of accommodative territory?

**A:**

The phrase "adjusting monetary accommodation" applies while policy is still accommodative and the BOJ is moving toward a neutral level.

When financial conditions are no longer accommodative, the BOJ will need a different policy framework and explanation. But the BOJ cannot say now when that point will be reached. It must judge through the actual process of raising rates and observing financial conditions.

---

### Q21. Is the rate hike consistent with government policy?

**Q:** The government is promoting domestic investment and using subsidies to reduce energy costs. Is BOJ tightening consistent with that?

**A:**

Yes. The government is trying to reduce downside risks to the economy and cushion households and firms from energy-price shocks, while also promoting medium- to long-term investment and resilience.

The BOJ's rate hike is intended to respond to broad price pressures and upside risk to underlying inflation, thereby supporting sustainable economic growth. Since financial conditions remain accommodative even after the hike, the BOJ believes its policy continues to support economic activity and investment.

---

### Q22. Is government fiscal policy working against BOJ tightening?

**Q:** The government continues expansionary fiscal policy. Is that inconsistent with BOJ policy?

**A:**

Fiscal policy is the responsibility of the government and parliament, so the BOJ does not comment directly.

The BOJ's role is to conduct monetary policy to achieve price stability.

---

### Q23. What is the status of the BOJ's JGB purchase reduction plan?

**Q:** Why did the BOJ decide on around JPY 2tn of monthly JGB purchases from April 2027 onward?

**A:**

The BOJ judged this based on market participant feedback and its own assessment.

JGB market functioning has improved steadily, so the need to reduce purchases further has declined. At the same time, as the BOJ reduces its holdings, other investors need time to absorb the bonds. A JPY 2tn monthly purchase pace allows the balance sheet to keep shrinking while avoiding unnecessary market disruption.

---

### Q24. Is the smaller JGB purchase reduction a concession to the government?

**Q:** Some say the slower JGB purchase reduction is a political consideration. Is that correct?

**A:**

No. The BOJ made the decision independently, after listening to market participants.

The decision balances JGB market stability and market functioning.

---

### Q25. Is slowing JGB purchase reduction inconsistent with raising rates?

**Q:** Is it contradictory to raise rates while slowing the reduction in JGB purchases?

**A:**

No. The short-term policy rate is the BOJ's main policy tool.

JGB purchases relate to the management of assets already acquired and are designed to run in the background without policy implications. Therefore, the BOJ does not see a contradiction.

---

### Q26. Could the BOJ revise the JGB purchase plan again?

**Q:** Under what conditions could the JGB purchase plan be revised?

**A:**

The BOJ has not decided how long the JPY 2tn pace will continue.

Potential factors include JGB market functioning, how domestic investors such as banks and households adjust their portfolios, and how the market reacts. The BOJ will consider predictability, but if conditions change, the Policy Board may revise the plan.

---

### Q27. What is the desired size of the BOJ balance sheet?

**Q:** What is the end-goal for the BOJ's balance sheet?

**A:**

At some point, the BOJ will need to discuss the appropriate size and composition of its balance sheet, including both assets and liabilities.

However, Japan still has an extremely large balance sheet, and the current priority is reducing it without disrupting the JGB market. Even by 2030, the balance sheet would only decline to the levels shown in the BOJ's projections, so it is still too early to define the final target.

---

### Q28. Will the BOJ speed up ETF sales?

**Q:** ETF sales have been underway for about six months. Could the BOJ accelerate the pace?

**A:**

At this point, the BOJ is not considering changing the current pace.

The current pace would take more than 100 years, so it is possible that it may be reviewed at some point. But less than one year has passed, and the BOJ does not see a major change in conditions requiring an immediate revision.

---

### Q29. What is the BOJ's view on past unconventional easing?

**Q:** How does the BOJ assess the long period of unconventional easing?

**A:**

Japan's deflationary mindset was deeply entrenched, and nominal interest rates were already very low, making monetary easing difficult.

Large-scale easing helped improve employment and created labour shortages, which contributed to the current environment where both wages and prices are rising. Therefore, it was effective in helping Japan escape deflation.

Now that this has been achieved, the BOJ is gradually exiting that extraordinary policy environment.

---

### Q30. What did Uchida mean by central bank "self-discipline"?

**Q:** In a previous speech, Uchida used the phrase "self-discipline" regarding central banking. What did he mean?

**A:**

He did not mean only unconventional policy. He meant that central banks must continue earning trust.

This applies to areas such as digital money, central bank balance sheets, and the future role of central bank money. People do not automatically trust central bank money simply because it is issued by a central bank. Maintaining that trust requires ongoing effort and discipline.

---

### Q31. What would the BOJ do in a financial crisis?

**Q:** If a financial crisis occurred, what should a central bank do?

**A:**

There is no single answer. The response depends on the nature of the shock: whether it is domestic or external, whether it is a liquidity problem, a confidence problem, or something affecting monetary policy transmission.

Possible responses include statements to reassure markets, liquidity provision, or broader monetary policy measures. In the past, BOJ liquidity operations and quantitative easing helped support financial system stability.

---

### Q32. Is the BOJ concerned about losses or negative equity?

**Q:** The BOJ's interest income and interest payments imply losses. Is this a serious risk?

**A:**

The BOJ has already published estimates showing the possibility of losses and even negative equity, but it does not see the risk as very large.

Central bank finances are important, but losses or negative equity do not necessarily prevent a central bank from conducting policy. Similar situations have already occurred at some overseas central banks.

The BOJ understood from the beginning that quantitative easing could generate profits in the early phase and losses later. That was part of the cost of the policy.
