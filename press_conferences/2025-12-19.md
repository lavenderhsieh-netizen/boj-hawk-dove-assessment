# BOJ Press Conference — 18–19 Dec 2025

**Decision:** Hike to 0.75%  
**Vote:** 9–0 (unanimous)  
**Presider:** Governor UEDA Kazuo

---

The BOJ raised the uncollateralized overnight call rate target from around
*0.5%* to around *0.75%*, by unanimous vote.

The BOJ judged that Japan’s economy is recovering moderately, although some
weak signs remain. Labor markets remain tight, corporate profits are
expected to stay high overall, and wage-setting behavior appears likely to
remain firm.

The BOJ also judged that uncertainty around the US economy and tariff
policy has declined, although it has not disappeared.

Underlying inflation continues to rise gradually. Recent data and hearing
information suggest that the mechanism in which wages and prices rise
together is likely to remain in place. Therefore, the BOJ judged that the
probability of achieving its central outlook — underlying inflation moving
broadly in line with the 2% target in the second half of the projection
period — has increased.

Even after the hike, real interest rates remain significantly negative, so
financial conditions remain accommodative.


## Q&A Transcript

### Q1. Why did the BOJ hike today?

**A:**

At the October meeting, we held rates because uncertainty around the US
economy and overseas developments remained high, and because we wanted to
confirm the initial momentum for next year’s wage negotiations.

Since then, US downside risks have declined. Tariff costs have only been
passed through gradually, personal consumption remains firm, and AI-related
demand continues to support business investment.

In Japan, tariff effects are weighing on manufacturing profits, but we do
not see them spreading broadly to capex, employment, or wages. The latest
Tankan showed corporate profit plans were revised up slightly, including in
manufacturing.

On wages, labor and management responses ahead of next year’s negotiations,
along with BOJ branch hearing information, suggest a high probability of
another solid wage increase.

Together with the gradual rise in underlying inflation, this means
confidence in our central outlook has increased. Therefore, we judged it
appropriate to raise rates and adjust the degree of monetary accommodation.

---

### Q2. What is the outlook for future rate hikes and the neutral rate?

**A:**

The future rate path and pace of adjustment will depend on economic, price,
and financial conditions. At each meeting, we will update our assessment of
the outlook, risks, and probability of realization.

Neutral-rate estimates vary widely, so it is difficult to identify the
level in advance. We need to adjust rates while observing how the economy
and prices respond to changes in short-term rates.

When assessing the degree of monetary accommodation, we will not look only
at the relationship between short-term rates and neutral rates. We will
also examine real interest rates, lending trends, and broader financial
conditions.

---

### Q3. What is the significance of the policy rate reaching the highest level in 30 years?

**A:**

I do not attach special meaning to the fact that the rate is now 0.75%, the
highest in 30 years.

The environment is very different from the past. Inflation is much higher
than when 0.5% had previously been the upper range. That said, because
0.75% is a 30-year high, we will carefully monitor its effects.

---

### Q4. Why does the BOJ still describe real rates as extremely low after the hike?

**A:**

One reason is that the policy rate is still somewhat below the lower end of
estimated neutral-rate ranges.

Also, when we look back at the hikes so far, including the exit from
negative rates, we do not see evidence of a very strong tightening effect
on the economy, prices, or financial conditions. Taking these points
together, real rates remain extremely low.

---

### Q5. How will the hike affect households, housing loans, and corporate borrowing?

**A:**

This hike reduces the degree of accommodation somewhat, but accommodative
conditions remain.

The impact will differ depending on borrowing maturity. Short- and
medium-term real rates remain low, while longer-term rates have risen more.
Therefore, some impact may appear in long-term corporate lending and
fixed-rate housing loans.

However, this should be judged against the backdrop of strong corporate
profits and rising wages.

---

### Q6. If the policy rate reaches 1%, would conditions still be accommodative?

**A:**

We cannot say mechanically because neutral-rate estimates are uncertain.

If rates were raised to 1%, we would judge whether conditions remain
accommodative by looking at how the economy, prices, and financial markets
respond to the 0.75% rate.

---

### Q7. Does the BOJ need to wait for lending or housing-loan data before hiking again?

**A:**

Lending data can move slowly, but we receive monthly information. We also
monitor bank lending attitudes, firms’ funding availability, bankruptcies,
and various financial-market indicators.

We will use all of these to assess how rate hikes are affecting financial
conditions.

---

### Q8. Did yen depreciation affect today’s rate decision?

**A:**

Several members noted that recent yen depreciation could push up import
prices and domestic prices, and could potentially affect underlying
inflation. That is something we need to monitor.

However, this was a risk to watch rather than a claim that yen depreciation
has already caused a large jump in headline inflation.

---

### Q9. Why did the BOJ publish a wide range of neutral-rate estimates?

**A:**

Neutral rate is an important concept for monetary policy. Other central
banks also estimate it and use it as a reference.

But our estimates have a very wide range, and we cannot say which estimate
is most plausible. That is why we published the range rather than narrowing
it artificially.

Going forward, we may update the estimates, but statistical work alone
cannot pin down the neutral rate. We also need to infer it by observing how
the economy responds to rate changes.

---

### Q10. How is communication with the government and the finance minister?

**A:**

Communication with Finance Minister Katayama has been close and good. More
broadly, the BOJ maintains communication with the government at many
levels, and we will continue making efforts.

---

### Q11. Did BOJ communication before this meeting reflect lessons from the July experience?

**A:**

We have tried to explain our basic policy thinking as clearly as possible.

Our current framework emphasizes underlying inflation rather than headline
inflation. Underlying inflation depends on whether the wage-price mechanism
continues and at what level.

From the previous meeting to now, the initial momentum for next year’s wage
negotiations became an important piece of information. I discussed this at
the previous press conference and again in my Nagoya speech because I
wanted to explain it carefully.

---

### Q12. Can the BOJ continue hiking even if headline inflation falls below 2%?

**A:**

Yes, it is possible.

We expect headline inflation to fall below 2% in the first half of next
year because food inflation should decline and government measures will
also lower inflation.

But policy focuses more on underlying inflation. If wages rise as expected
and wage increases continue passing through to prices, underlying inflation
should not decline in the same way as headline inflation. If that process
continues to be confirmed, further hikes at an appropriate timing are
possible.

---

### Q13. How does the BOJ view long-term JGB yields rising above 2%?

**A:**

I will avoid commenting on short-term market moves. Long-term rates are
basically formed in the market.

They reflect views on the economy, prices, monetary policy, fiscal policy,
and overseas long-term rates. We will monitor them carefully.

If market moves become exceptional and different from normal functioning,
the BOJ can conduct flexible operations if necessary.

---

### Q14. Is Japan currently in an inflationary state?

**A:**

Yes, in the sense that headline inflation and underlying inflation have
been above zero for the past few years.

But our aim is not simply to have inflation. Our aim is to guide inflation
to the 2% target in a sustainable and stable manner.

---

### Q15. Does today’s positive assessment imply multiple future hikes?

**A:**

The decline in downside risks and the increased probability of the outlook
being realized led to today’s hike.

But the future path depends on incoming data and information at future
meetings. I cannot pre-commit to multiple hikes.

---

### Q16. What are the main risks to the outlook?

**A:**

US tariff policy remains a risk, though the size of the risk has declined.
So far, US firms have absorbed much of the tariff cost, but if more is
passed on to consumers, US consumption and Japanese exports could be
affected.

China’s slowdown is also a risk, although we expect some policy response if
conditions worsen significantly.

For Japan, we are watching whether small and medium-sized firms can keep up
with higher wages, higher inflation, and rising interest rates.

---

### Q17. Is the BOJ’s JGB purchase-reduction plan contributing to higher long-term yields?

**A:**

The BOJ’s large JGB holdings still exert a stock effect that pushes yields
down.

Markets also know the future path of purchase reductions, so some decline
in the stock effect may already be priced in.

We plan to review the purchase-reduction path at the regular review next
summer, and if truly necessary, an interim review is possible.

---

### Q18. Could food inflation become part of underlying inflation?

**A:**

Yes, part of food-price inflation can become related to underlying
inflation.

If wages continue rising at a pace consistent with 2% inflation,
food-related firms will face higher labor costs and may pass those costs
into prices. Some firms already cite this as one reason for food-price
increases.

That component can be considered part of underlying inflation.

---

### Q19. Is the BOJ at risk of moving too slowly and fueling inflation?

**A:**

The BOJ needs to judge rate hikes at the appropriate timing so that
inflation lands smoothly and sustainably at 2%.

We will continue making appropriate decisions to achieve that.

---

### Q20. How does the BOJ assess wage prospects at SMEs?

**A:**

We have tried to assess SME wage momentum through labor-management
information and BOJ branch hearings.

We see some forward-looking developments among SMEs. Corporate profits at
SMEs also do not look weak overall based on corporate statistics.

The issue is variation across industries and very small firms. We will keep
monitoring those areas closely.

---

### Q21. Why did the BOJ need to hike now rather than wait longer?

**A:**

In general, if rate hikes are delayed too much, central banks can later be
forced into very large hikes. That can be very damaging for the economy and
financial markets.

Avoiding that risk is one reason for acting at the appropriate timing.

---

### Q22. Is there a risk that the BOJ hikes above neutral because neutral is uncertain?

**A:**

That risk cannot be denied.

But all central banks face this problem. Neutral rates cannot be precisely
identified, yet central banks still need to make policy decisions.

We will reduce the risk of mistakes as much as possible by examining data
carefully, especially how the economy and financial conditions respond to
rate changes.

---

### Q23. Will future hikes also depend on spring wage negotiations?

**A:**

Wages are an important point because they are central to the wage-price
mechanism.

The last two hikes happened to focus heavily on wage developments. But
future decisions will also depend on how wages pass through to prices and
how underlying inflation evolves.

We need to distinguish between temporary price movements and underlying
inflation trends.

---

### Q24. Could yen depreciation force the BOJ to accelerate hikes?

**A:**

I will not comment on short-term exchange-rate moves.

But yen depreciation could affect prices, especially underlying inflation,
because firms’ price-setting behavior has become more active. We will
monitor that carefully.

---

### Q25. Does “real rates remain extremely low” imply at least one more hike?

**A:**

The remaining room for hikes depends on how today’s hike affects economic,
price, and financial conditions.

We will assess those effects carefully before deciding.

---

### Q26. Is the BOJ moving away from Abenomics?

**A:**

We see ourselves as entering the final stage of achieving the goals set
under the government-BOJ joint statement.

There have been side effects, and some may still emerge. Our aim is to
complete the final stage as smoothly as possible.

---

### Q27. What matters most for estimating neutral rates?

**A:**

More data helps. It is also easier to estimate neutral rates if the economy
has gone through repeated cycles above and below neutral, but Japan has not
had many such cycles over the past 30 years.

Neutral rates are also closely related to potential growth, so better
understanding potential growth is important.

---

### Q28. Which real interest rate does the BOJ mean when it says real rates are extremely low?

**A:**

The answer depends on which inflation expectation measure is used.

But even using various measures, the overnight policy rate and short-term
rates up to around two or three years are clearly and significantly
negative in real terms.

---

### Q29. Could the current 0.75% rate already be neutral?

**A:**

Statistical estimates alone cannot determine the neutral rate.

We need to proceed by observing how the economy responds to interest-rate
changes. In that sense, we are moving forward while searching for the
neutral rate.

---

### Q30. What explains the structural yen weakness since 2022?

**A:**

Interest-rate differentials have clearly had a large impact over this
period.

There may also be structural factors, but I do not have a precise estimate
of what they are or how much they contributed.

---

### Q31. Is the return of “first-force” inflation positive or negative for reaching 2%?

**A:**

The first force is often temporary and fades over time.

However, it can spill over into the second force if it affects inflation
expectations or wage growth. That transmission is possible and needs to be
watched.

---

### Q32. Will the BOJ continue hiking gradually and avoid falling behind the curve?

**A:**

The pace of hikes will depend on incoming data and economic conditions.

Using your words, we will conduct policy so that we do not fall behind the
curve — meaning that inflation lands smoothly at the 2% target.
